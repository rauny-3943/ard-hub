1. History of ideas by theme

Common ground: mechanisms, first established for mine drainage

The foundational works in the corpus are not about mountains but about mine drainage, and they set the mechanisms later applied to natural ARD. [[Singer1970]] identify the oxidation of ferrous to ferric iron as the rate-limiting step; [[Moses1987]] conclude that Fe(III) is the direct oxidant of pyrite; [[Williamson1994]] provide the reference rate laws. The mineralogy of secondary precipitates is set out by [[Bigham1990]], then [[Bigham1996]] (the jarosite – schwertmannite – ferrihydrite – goethite sequence) and [[Bigham2000]], and the role of microorganisms by [[Nordstrom1997]]. [[Nordstrom1999]], the most cited mechanism reference in the corpus, and [[Plumlee1999]] bridge mining and natural processes; Plumlee already stresses the need to estimate pre-mining water composition. More recent reviews ([[Nordstrom2011]], [[Blowes2013]], [[Dold2014]], [[Nordstrom2015]], [[Dold2016]], [[ParbhakarFox2016]], [[Schoepfer2021]]) consolidate this base. [[Raiswell2009]] show that schwertmannite-bearing micro-scale ARD exists beneath glaciers themselves.

Hydrochemistry (the core of the corpus): from “background” natural ARD to climate-driven ARD

First phase (1990s–2000s): natural ARD as geochemical background. In the mineralised terrains of the Rocky Mountains, [[Bassett1992]] model natural acid-sulfate weathering at Geneva Creek, [[Miller1994]] show that some altered areas produce acid drainage comparable to mine drainage (pH down to 2.5), and [[Meyer1996]], [[Bird2005]] and [[Sares2006]] inventory such occurrences in Colorado. In Yukon and Alaska, [[Kwong2006]], [[Kwong2008]] and [[Graham2008]] describe naturally acidic streams on black shales or outcropping deposits. The issue is then regulatory: separating natural from mining contributions, a line followed by [[Downing2014]], [[Runkel2018]] (estimated pre-mining concentrations already above standards at Cinnamon Gulch) and [[Jones2023]].

First turning point (2005–2013): climate comes in. Two papers, the most cited in the corpus, shift the question. [[Williams2005]] describe the sulfate-rich outflow of a Front Range rock glacier; [[Thies2007]] report an 18-fold increase in conductivity and nickel above standards in an Alpine lake, an “unexpected” response to warming. [[Nordstrom2008]] formalise the link between ARD and climate change (the “first flush” after dry periods); [[Mast2010]] observe rising sulfate in weathering-dominated lakes while atmospheric deposition declines. In the upper Snake River, [[Todd2012]], [[Crouch2012]] and [[Manning2013]] relate a 100–400% increase in low-flow zinc and sulfate to warming, earlier snowmelt and a falling water table. This line leads to [[Manning2024]]: in 17 acidic streams across 22 mineralised catchments, sulfate, zinc and copper nearly doubled in 30 years. [[Crawford2019]] generalise the idea of accelerated bedrock weathering.

Second turning point (2014–2025): rock glaciers and mountain permafrost. The Alps become the main laboratory. [[Ilyashuk2014]] identify an active rock glacier as a source of ARD with measurable biological effects (deformities in chironomid larvae); the review by [[Colombo2017]] structures the field and sets out a research agenda, complemented by [[Jones2019]] on rock glacier hydrology. Studies of springs and ponds follow ([[Colombo2018b]], [[Colombo2019]], [[Thies2018]], [[Steingruber2020]], [[Brighenti2021]], [[Bearzot2023]], [[DelSiro2023]]), then the demonstration of full ARD on pyrite-bearing crystalline rocks ([[Wanner2023]], [[Moradi2024]], [[Kohler2025]], [[Seelig2025]]) and experiments on the role of freeze–thaw ([[Ilyashuk2024]]). [[Brighenti2024]] scale up to 201 springs (35% of intact rock glacier springs fail drinking-water standards) and [[Brighenti2025]] to a large river network (Ni up to ×4 in ten years). [[Nickus2023]] add a multi-millennial perspective with the ice chemistry of a rock glacier.

Third turning point (2023–2026): abrupt ARD from Arctic permafrost thaw. [[ODonnell2024]] document the “rusting” of dozens of Brooks Range streams within 5 to 10 years. [[Skierszkan2024b]] frame it as an emerging risk; [[Skierszkan2026]], [[Skierszkan2026b]], [[Evinger2026]] and [[Grasby2026]] describe abrupt acidification (pH from 7.5 to 2.7 within a few years at one Yukon site). [[Walsh2024]] and, at the global scale, [[Torres2017]] link sulfide oxidation to the carbon cycle.

Geomorphology: a thin but structuring theme

Only 10 references have geomorphology as their main theme, but 55 have it as a secondary theme. The oldest thread treats ferricretes as archives: [[Furniss1999]] date natural ARD back to 8,840 years BP, and [[Maza2014]] describe a Holocene ochreous palaeo-lake in the Famatina. In the Cordillera Blanca, conference abstracts call for remapping the sulfide-bearing rocks exposed by glacier retreat ([[Patel2017]], [[Vaughn2019]], [[Williams2020]]), [[Emmer2019]] date deglaciation in the Churup valley and [[Navas2022]] trace the sources of proglacial fine sediment. The turning point comes from the Pyrenees: [[Zarroca2021]] combine aerial photographs (1945–2018) with water chemistry and relate the upward shift of ARD (45–55 m per decade) to that of periglacial limits; [[Guerra2026]] then link rock slope failures, acid springs and ferricretes. Remote sensing extends this theme: [[Garcia2025]] and [[SantiagoBazan2026]] in the Cordillera Blanca, [[Barker2024]] in the Arctic.

Soil: the most recent and thinnest theme

The 12 “Soil & ARD” references all date from 2010 or later (median 2023), and three are known only at the metadata level ([[Bacardit2010]], [[Schulze2017]], [[Zimmer2020]]). They follow three approaches: proglacial pedogenesis, where [[Zimmer2023c]] show that parent rock matters more than soil age and flag a toxicity risk (As, Cu, Mo, Mn) in Andean soils, together with [[Zimmer2023b]] and, in the Pyrenees, [[Vidaller2025]] (which does not address acidity); the biogeochemistry of permafrost soils ([[Barker2023]], [[Skierszkan2024]], [[Colombo2020]]) and peatlands ([[Miller2025]]); and contaminated soils and their flora ([[CruzadoTafur2020]]). [[CastilloVergara2026]] is the only study in the corpus that compares soils and waters between a valley with an ARD signature (Gueshgue) and a neutral one (Llaca) in the Cordillera Blanca.

Other: ecology, health, context

Biological consequences are studied mainly in the Alps and the Andes: Andean macroinvertebrates ([[LoayzaMuro2012]], [[LoayzaMuro2013b]]), Alpine diatoms and invertebrates ([[Rotta2018]], [[Ilyashuk2017]], [[Brighenti2019]]), microorganisms ([[GonzalezToril2015]] at Pastoruri, [[Bernardelli2020]], [[GuijosaOrtega2025]] in the Pyrenees). The effect of ARD on vegetation remains rarely studied: [[Arp1999]] (Rocky Mountain fens, mining context), [[AliagaZegarra2009]] and [[OtoyaFernandez2026]] (Áncash wetlands), [[Shepherd2023]] (forests, metadata only). Human health only appears in 2024 with [[BravoZevallos2024]], then [[MoscosoMujica2026]], both in the Negro River sub-basin. The context references of the initial bibliography ([[Rabatel2013]], [[Seehaus2019]], [[Vilimek2024]], [[Barrow2023]], [[Feng2024]]…) frame glaciology, climate and plant nutrition.

2. Comparison between regions

The table below is computed from the corpus. It shows a literature dominated by three hubs (Cordillera Blanca, Rocky Mountains, Alps) that share neither the same trigger nor the same history.

Cordillera Blanca: a recent, dense and mostly spatial hub

The corpus contains work from 2007 onwards (wetland bioremediation, [[Cadenas2007]]; Santa River sediments, [[Rivera2008]]), but the demonstration of natural ARD comparable to mine drainage in a catchment without mines comes from the Quillcayhuanca valley ([[Burns2011]], [[Fortner2011]]: pH mostly < 4, Fe up to 21 mg/L in the main stem). The trigger invoked is almost always glacier retreat, which exposes the pyritic shales of the Chicama Formation ([[Burns2011]], [[Chira2022]], [[BravoZevallos2024]], [[Garcia2025]]). [[Santofimia2017]] characterise ARD at Pastoruri (lakes at pH ≈ 3, schwertmannite and goethite controlling iron, recovery to pH 6.9 5.5 km downstream). Studies then widen to the scale of the range: 7 valleys ([[Bain2020]]), 18 catchments ([[Macdonald2021]]), 267 lakes mapped with Landsat, of which 60 are very acidic ([[Garcia2025]]), probabilistic mapping ([[SantiagoBazan2026]]). The Santa basin mixes natural and mining sources ([[Walsh2013]], [[Guittard2017]], [[Guittard2020]]). Not every valley is affected: the Ulta valley is weakly mineralised ([[Magnusson2020]]) and Llaca stays neutral next to Gueshgue ([[CastilloVergara2026]]). The region stands out for ecotoxicology and health ([[CrisantoRojas2023]], [[BravoZevallos2024]], [[MoscosoMujica2026]]) and for plant-based remediation trials ([[AliagaZegarra2009]], [[OtoyaFernandez2026]]). Its weakness is time: many studies rely on a single sampling campaign (explicitly so for [[Magnusson2020]], [[BravoZevallos2024]] and [[MoscosoMujica2026]]), with no long series comparable to those of the Rocky Mountains or the Alps.

Pyrenees: few references, but one pivotal paper

The corpus holds only 10 Pyrenean references, of which only one was read in full text ([[Serrano2012]], the permafrost map). The oldest are background references, known only from their metadata: chemistry of high-altitude lakes ([[Catalan1993]]), fossil ferricretes ([[Wright1992]]), high-altitude soils ([[Bacardit2010]]). [[Zaharescu2009]] attribute the trace metals of Lake Respomuso to a geogenic source (sulfides in slates), and [[Zaharescu2016]] relate rising metal contents in a lake sediment core to climate change since the 1980s. [[Zarroca2021]] is the pivotal paper: the length of affected streams grew from about 5 km (1945) to more than 35 km (2018), with white aluminium precipitates and a role for rock glaciers, scree and deep-seated gravitational slope deformation. The papers that follow all date from 2025–2026: microbiology ([[GuijosaOrtega2025]]), proglacial soils of the Maladeta massif ([[Vidaller2025]]), rock slope failures and ferricretes ([[Guerra2026]]). Unlike the Cordillera Blanca, the trigger invoked is warming and drought, acting through periglacial landforms and slope instability, rather than direct glacier retreat.

What the comparison shows

The two priority regions share a substrate (pyritic pelites: slates of the Axial Zone, Chicama Formation) and tools (aerial or satellite imagery, molecular microbiology), but not the same visible products: white aluminium precipitates in the Pyrenees ([[Zarroca2021]]), iron oxyhydroxysulfates at Pastoruri ([[Santofimia2017]]). They rarely cite each other: the graph contains only citation links between them. The Alps provide what both lack: long lake time series ([[Thies2007]], [[Rogora2019]], [[Schreder2023]]), rock glacier spring budgets and sulfur isotopes ([[Steingruber2020]]). The Rocky Mountains provide the method for separating natural from mining sources and 30-year trends ([[Manning2024]]). The Arctic shows that ARD can appear within a few years ([[Skierszkan2026]]).

3. Gaps and directions for the internship

  • Soil is the weakest link. Only twelve references, nearly all published after 2017, and no Pyrenean study links soils directly to acidity ([[Vidaller2025]] does not address it). A soil transect along an ARD gradient (pH, exchangeable Al and Fe, metals, nutrients), modelled on [[CastilloVergara2026]], would fill a gap in both regions.
  • Vegetation under natural ARD is almost unexplored. The corpus holds only a few studies, often in a mining context ([[Bech2011]], [[ChangKee2018]], [[CruzadoTafur2021]]) or focused on remediation ([[OtoyaFernandez2026]]). The context references of the initial bibliography on plant traits and nutrition ([[Delhaye2020]], [[Delpiano2020]], [[Feng2024]], [[Barrow2023]], [[Hartemink2023]], [[Yadav2020]]) have no counterpart in the corpus applied to natural mountain ARD: measuring functional traits along an acidity gradient would be a new contribution.
  • Time series are missing in the Cordillera Blanca and the Pyrenees. There is no long chemical series comparable to those of the Alps or the Rocky Mountains. Repeating measurements in the dry and wet seasons (or before and after snowmelt), with concentration–discharge relationships, would test the “first flush” effect of [[Nordstrom2008]] and the seasonal controls described by [[Colombo2018b]].
  • Tools to transfer. The spectral index of [[Garcia2025]] and the probabilistic mapping of [[SantiagoBazan2026]] have no Pyrenean equivalent in the corpus; the aerial-photograph approach of [[Zarroca2021]] has not been applied to other Pyrenean valleys. The permafrost map of [[Serrano2012]] provides a basis for crossing periglacial landforms with acid springs.
  • The origin of sulfate. Sulfur isotopes, used in the Alps ([[Steingruber2020]]) and in Yukon ([[VanStempvoort2023]]) to distinguish sulfides from gypsum, appear in no Pyrenean study in the corpus.
  • Priority reading. Two references of the initial bibliography, [[Zarroca2021]] and [[Santofimia2017]], were read only at the abstract level; [[Taucare2025]] only through a press release. Some references that look central remain at the metadata level: [[Downing2014b]] (ARD, permafrost and glaciers), [[Catalan1993]], [[Rogora2024]], [[Martin2024]]. Getting them through the library is the first practical step.
